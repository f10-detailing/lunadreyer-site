How a document travels from here to there

The order matters. Translating too early, before the wording is settled, is the most common reason a document is refused.

  1. Settle what the document must say

    The receiving authority or lawyer sets the requirements. This is decided first, not after signing.

  2. Translation

    The document is prepared in both languages, so what is signed in Australia is what is read in Europe.

  3. Notarisation

    A public notary witnesses the signature and applies their seal.

  4. Apostille

    The Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade legalises the notary's seal for use in Hague Convention countries.

  5. Lodged abroad

    The document reaches the lawyer, registry or authority in a form they can act on.

About the agency

Luna Dreyer Translation was founded by Luna Dreyer, who holds a Bachelor of Arts in Übersetzungswissenschaft from Heidelberg University, in German, English and Spanish, and also works in Italian and Portuguese. Heidelberg's translation institute was the first of its kind in Europe, and its training is built around precisely this kind of work: legal and official text where precision is the entire job.

Our collaborators cover Greek (including Cypriot Greek), Italian, Maltese and French, so one agency can handle a matter from Australia to most of southern Europe.

We do not provide NAATI-certified translations. NAATI is the Australian system, and it is what you need for foreign documents going to Australian departments. For documents leaving Australia, European authorities apply their own requirements, which vary by country and by the office receiving the document. If you are unsure which applies to you, ask us before you pay for anything.
