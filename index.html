How a document travels from here to there

The order matters. Translating too early, before the wording is settled, is the most common reason a document is refused.

  1. Settle what the document must say

    The receiving authority or lawyer sets the requirements. This is decided first, not after signing.

  2. Translation

    The document is prepared in both languages, so what is signed in Australia is what is read in Europe.

  3. Notarisation

    A public notary witnesses the signature and applies their seal.

  4. Apostille

    The Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade legalises the notary's seal for use in Hague Convention countries.

  5. Lodged abroad

    The document reaches the lawyer, registry or authority in a form they can act on.

About

I hold a Bachelor of Arts in Ubersetzungswissenschaft from Heidelberg University, in German, English and Spanish. I also work in Italian and Portuguese. Heidelberg's translation institute was the first of its kind in Europe, and its training is built around precisely this kind of work: legal and official text where precision is the entire job.

I am not NAATI certified. NAATI is the Australian system, and it is what you need for foreign documents going to Australian departments. For documents leaving Australia, European authorities apply their own requirements, which vary by country and by the office receiving the document. If you are unsure which applies to you, ask me before you pay for anything.
