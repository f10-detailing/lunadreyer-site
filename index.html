Ask me

How a document gets from here to there

The order matters. Translating too early, or before the wording is settled, is the most common reason a document comes back.

  1. Settle what the document must say

    The receiving authority or lawyer sets the requirements. This is decided first, not after signing.

  2. Translation

    The document is prepared in both languages, so what is signed in Australia is what is read in Europe.

  3. Notarisation

    A public notary witnesses the signature and applies their seal.

  4. Apostille

    The Department of Foreign Affairs and Trade legalises the notary's seal for use in Hague Convention countries.

  5. Lodged abroad

    The document reaches the lawyer, registry or authority in a form they can act on.

About

I hold a BA in Übersetzungswissenschaft from the University of Heidelberg, in German, English and Spanish. Heidelberg's translation faculty is one of the oldest in Europe, and the training is built around exactly this kind of work: legal and official text where precision is the whole job. I also work in Italian and Portuguese.

I am not NAATI certified. NAATI is the Australian system, and it is what you need for foreign documents going to Australian departments. For documents leaving Australia, European authorities look to their own requirements instead, which vary by country and by the authority receiving the document. If you are unsure which applies to you, ask me before you pay for anything.
