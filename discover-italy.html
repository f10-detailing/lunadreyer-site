An Italian summer, where the family story began.

While Melbourne pulls on its coat, the piazzas of Sicily, Calabria, Veneto, Abruzzo, Friuli and Campania are warm until late at night. These are the towns so many Melbourne families came from, and the places Australians keep going back to, for a season or for good.

Six places to come home to

From the Ionian coast of Sicily to the Adriatic in the north, each of these places has deep roots in Melbourne's Italian community, and each one is at its best in the European summer.

Sicily

Taormina

Perched on a terrace above the Ionian Sea, Taormina looks straight across the bay to Mount Etna. Summer evenings mean a stroll along Corso Umberto, a concert in the ancient Greek theatre and almond granita with brioche, with the little island of Isola Bella waiting in the cove below.

Calabria

Tropea and the Costa degli Dei

Tropea's old town sits on a sandstone cliff above white sand and clear turquoise water, with the sanctuary of Santa Maria dell'Isola on its own rock just offshore. Along the Coast of the Gods you swim in hidden coves around Capo Vaticano, watch Stromboli on the horizon at sunset and eat the famous sweet red onions of Tropea.

Veneto

Verona and Lake Garda

Every summer, opera fills Verona's Roman Arena under the stars, as it has for more than a century. Half an hour away, Lake Garda, Italy's largest lake, offers lakeside towns like Bardolino, Lazise and Sirmione, ferries between olive groves and lemon gardens, and a glass of local wine as the sun drops behind the hills.

Friuli Venezia Giulia

Trieste

A grand port city where Italian, Central European and Slavic cultures meet, with Piazza Unità d'Italia opening right onto the sea. Mornings start in historic coffee houses, afternoons are spent swimming off the Barcola promenade like the locals, and the white castle of Miramare glows on the shore.

Abruzzo

Costa dei Trabocchi

South of Ortona, wooden fishing platforms called trabocchi stand on stilts above a calm Adriatic, and several now serve seafood dinners right over the water. Cycle the Via Verde along the old coastal railway, swim from pebbly coves near San Vito Chietino and Fossacesia, and see the Maiella mountains rise behind you.

Campania

Positano and the Amalfi Coast

Pastel houses tumble down the cliffs to the Spiaggia Grande, beneath the majolica dome of Santa Maria Assunta. Summer days here mean boats to Amalfi and Capri, lemon granita in the shade and the Path of the Gods walk high above the sea.

Summer there, while Melbourne is in winter

June to August

The heart of the Italian summer: long sunny days, a warm sea from Sicily to the Adriatic and towns full of life. It is the perfect swap for a Melbourne winter.

Long evenings

The light lingers until well after 8 pm. Italians take the evening passeggiata, meet friends for an aperitivo and sit down to dinner late, often outdoors in the piazza.

Sagre and Ferragosto

Summer is festival season, with village sagre celebrating everything from fish to wine. Ferragosto on 15 August is the high point of the holiday, and the grape harvest begins in many regions from late August.

Living there

Days in Italy follow the food. Weekly markets bring fruit, cheese and fish from nearby farms and boats, the local bar is where the day starts with an espresso at the counter, and lunch is still the meal that matters. In smaller towns, shops close in the early afternoon and the streets come back to life as the heat eases.

Many Australians who spend the season in Italy are reconnecting with the towns their parents or grandparents left. Cousins, village festivals and the parish piazza make it easy to feel part of the community, and returning each summer quickly turns neighbours into friends.

Getting around is simple. High speed and regional trains link the major cities, ferries cross the lakes and connect the islands, and a car opens up the hill villages and coastal roads. From Melbourne, the trip is usually one stop via a hub in Asia or the Middle East, into Rome, Milan or Venice, with easy connections to Catania, Lamezia, Naples, Trieste or Pescara.

Common questions

When is the best time to go?

June to September. June and early July are warm, green and relaxed, August is the full Italian summer with Ferragosto and the village festivals, and September brings the grape harvest, warm seas and a gentler pace.

Which area suits a quiet retirement, and which suits city life?

For a quiet life, the small towns of the Costa dei Trabocchi, the Costa degli Dei around Tropea and the villages on Lake Garda are hard to beat. For city life, Verona and Trieste combine theatres, cafes and good transport with an easy, walkable scale.

Is it easy to get by in English?

Yes, in the visitor areas, the larger towns and with younger Italians. A few words of Italian go a long way in village shops and markets, and many Australians with Italian family find the language comes back quickly once they are there.

Is it possible to rent for a whole season?

Yes. Apartments and village houses are commonly rented by the month, and booking early for July and August gives the widest choice. A season of renting is also the best way to find the town you want to stay in for good.
