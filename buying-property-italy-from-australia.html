Property in Italy

Buying property in Italy from Australia, without flying there twice.

Australians buy in Italy every year, whether a family house in the south, a place to retire to, or a lot at a court auction. The obstacle is rarely the money. It is that Italian property law runs on documents, in Italian, signed in person, and you are twenty hours away.

What you need before anything else

Codice fiscale

An Italian tax code. You cannot buy property, open an account or sign a deed without one. It is free, and obtained through an Italian consulate or a representative in Italy.

An Italian bank account

Not always compulsory, but it makes payment of the deposit, taxes and utilities far simpler than transferring from Australia each time.

Someone who can act for you

An avvocato or a trusted representative in Italy. For an auction this is not optional. Someone has to be present.

A power of attorney

The procura granting your representative authority to sign. Prepared and legalised in Australia, in a form an Italian notaio or court will accept.

A private sale, step by step

  1. Proposta di acquisto

    A written offer, usually with a small deposit. Binding on you once accepted, so we translate it for you before you sign and you know exactly what you are agreeing to.

  2. Compromesso

    The preliminary contract. This is where most of the money is committed, so we translate it into English before you sign. Deposit typically 10 to 20 percent.

  3. Power of attorney, if you are not attendingDone by us

    We draft it to the notaio's requirements and translate it, book the Australian Notary Public so you only need to attend and sign, usually in the same week, and take it to DFAT ourselves for the apostille.

  4. Rogito

    The final deed, signed before an Italian notaio, who registers the transfer and collects the taxes.

Judicial auctions

Court-ordered sales are published on the Portale delle Vendite Pubbliche, the Ministry of Justice auction portal, and are open to foreign buyers. Prices often sit below market because the court is recovering a debt rather than marketing a home.

They follow strict rules, and knowing them in advance is what makes an auction purchase run smoothly.

  • A deposit, the cauzione, is lodged before the auction and completion follows on a fixed timetable, so we have your power of attorney and documents ready before you bid
  • The perizia, the court surveyor's report, is the main description of the property and is written in Italian, so we translate it into English for you
  • The perizia also shows whether the property is occupied, as clearing it is a separate legal process with its own timeline, and our translation makes this clear before you decide
  • Bids are final, with no cooling off, conditional offer or renegotiation, which is why we translate the documents well before auction day
  • Bidding takes place in Italy, and a power of attorney is required for anyone not present, so we prepare it to the court's requirements

Where we fit

We translate the perizia, the auction notice and the supporting documents, and prepare the power of attorney so your lawyer can bid and complete on your behalf.

Common questions

Can Australians buy property in Italy?

Yes. Australians can buy property in Italy under the reciprocity principle. Italian citizenship is not required, and buying property does not grant residency or citizenship on its own.

Do you have to travel to Italy?

Not if you appoint someone to act for you. That is what the procura is for. Many buyers travel once to see the area, and handle the signing remotely.

What documents will need translating?

Typically the power of attorney and your identity documents, and on the Italian side the perizia, the visura catastale and the contract itself so that you understand what you are agreeing to.

What about the one euro houses?

Real, but the price is the smallest part. Councils generally require a renovation commitment within a fixed period and a guarantee deposit. The paperwork burden is the same as any other purchase.

Can you work alongside your Italian lawyer?

Yes. If you already have an avvocato, we work to their requirements, so the power of attorney and translations arrive in the form they need. If you do not have one yet, we can explain what to look for.
