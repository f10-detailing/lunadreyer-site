Power of attorney in Victoria

Power of attorney in Victoria: which kind do you need?

People in Victoria searching for a power of attorney usually need one of two quite different things. One is an enduring power of attorney for their own affairs here. The other is a document that has to be accepted by a notary, registry, bank or court overseas. They are made differently, signed differently, and one does not stand in for the other.

Two different documents

An enduring power of attorney, for Victoria

A statutory document made on a prescribed form under the Powers of Attorney Act 2014. It lets someone you choose act for you, and it is designed to keep working if you lose capacity. It is for use in Victoria.

Office of the Public Advocate

A power of attorney for use overseas

A document prepared for a specific foreign authority. It is signed before an Australian Notary Public, usually apostilled by DFAT, and often translated. The wording is set by the lawyer or notary who will use it overseas.

How the overseas route works

The Victorian enduring power of attorney, briefly

The Office of the Public Advocate sets out the basics. The main points:

  • You must be 18 or older and have the capacity to make it, and you can only make one for yourself. A carer or family member cannot make one for you.
  • You can appoint up to four attorneys, and an alternative for each.
  • Each attorney must accept the role in writing on the form.
  • It is signed in front of two eligible adult witnesses present together. One must be a medical practitioner or a person authorised to witness affidavits.
  • There is no central register. The usual practice is to give each attorney a certified copy.
  • Use the current prescribed forms, available from the Department of Justice and Community Safety.

The current form sets out who can and cannot witness. If the document you need is for use overseas, that is the route we coordinate, set out below.

If the document is going to Europe

A power of attorney that works in Victoria is not automatically accepted overseas. A European notary, registry or bank may want a document in its own format, and acceptance is decided by the receiving authority. DFAT's advice is to ask the overseas authority what it needs first, so we confirm that before anything is paid for or signed.

For Italy, Germany, Spain, Portugal and Greece the usual route runs in this order:

  1. Settle what the document must say

    The receiving lawyer or notary sets the wording. This is decided first, not after signing.

  2. TranslationDone by us

    The document is prepared in both languages, so what you sign in Victoria is what is read in Europe.

  3. Signing before an Australian Notary PublicDone by us

    We book the Notary Public, usually in the same week, so you only need to attend and sign. DFAT apostilles a private document once a Notary Public has notarised it, which is why a Justice of the Peace is not used.

  4. Apostille from DFATDone by us

    We take the signed document to DFAT ourselves, and DFAT legalises the notary's seal for use in Hague Convention countries.

What we do is coordinate the translation and the steps around it, so they happen in the right order. For the detail, see power of attorney with apostille in Australia, the apostille guide, and how recognition works in Europe.

Common questions

Can a Victorian enduring power of attorney be used in Italy or another European country?

Not automatically. An enduring power of attorney made on the Victorian form is designed for use in Victoria. Acceptance overseas is decided by the receiving lawyer, notary, registry or bank, so we confirm what they need before you sign and prepare a power of attorney for use overseas that follows it.

Is a Justice of the Peace enough for a power of attorney going overseas?

Usually not. A private document such as a power of attorney generally needs to be notarised by an Australian Notary Public before DFAT can apostille it. We coordinate the notary, the apostille and the translation in the right order.

Can you help with a power of attorney for property or an estate in Europe?

Yes. We coordinate a power of attorney for use overseas: prepared in both languages with wording set by the receiving lawyer or notary, signed before an Australian Notary Public in Victoria, and apostilled by DFAT.

How much does the overseas route cost?

Three separate costs: the Notary Public (set by the notary), the DFAT apostille (A$105 from 1 January 2026) and translation (which depends on the document).

How long does it take?

Allow one to three weeks end to end. The variable is usually how quickly the receiving lawyer confirms the wording.
