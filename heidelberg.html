Alma mater

Heidelberg University, and the training behind this work.

Clients rarely ask where a translator studied. They ask whether the document will be accepted. Those turn out to be the same question, because what a European authority is testing is whether the person who prepared the text understood the legal system it was written for.

The oldest university in Germany

Heidelberg University, formally the Ruprecht Karl University of Heidelberg, was founded in 1386. It is the oldest university in Germany and among the oldest in continuous operation in Europe, and it remains one of the country's leading research institutions.

For a translator the relevant part is not the age but the tradition it produced: a philological culture in which a text is read closely, in its own legal and historical context, before a single word of it is rendered into another language.

A legal document is not a set of words to be exchanged for other words. It is an instrument that has to do something, in a system with its own rules.

The Institut fur Ubersetzen und Dolmetschen

The translation institute where I studied has an unusual history. It was founded in 1929 at the Handelshochschule Mannheim as the first interpreting institute in Europe. When that school was dissolved in 1933 the institute was absorbed into the philosophical faculty at Heidelberg, and it has been part of the university ever since, today within the Faculty of Modern Languages.

It remains the only university-level translation studies institution in Baden-Wurttemberg.

Bachelor of Arts, Ubersetzungswissenschaft

Six semesters. German as the A language, with two further working languages chosen from English, French, Italian, Portuguese, Russian and Spanish. Mine were English and Spanish.

Translation studies, not language study

The degree is in the discipline of translation itself: theory, terminology, text analysis and specialised translation, rather than language learning. Working fluency is assumed on entry.

Specialised fields

The institute builds its teaching around specialised domains, with law, economics, medicine and technology among them. This is where legal translation stops being a matter of vocabulary.

An international faculty

A substantial share of students come from outside Germany, and the institute maintains exchange arrangements with partner universities across Europe and beyond.

Why this matters for your document

Three things from that training show up in every file I take on.

The source system is read first

A procura speciale, a Vollmacht and a power of attorney are not three names for one instrument. They sit in different legal traditions and grant authority in different ways. Translating the words without reading the system is how documents come back refused.

Terminology is researched, not guessed

Legal terminology work means establishing what a term does in its own system before choosing an equivalent, and flagging it where no equivalent exists rather than papering over the gap.

The reader is a specific office

A document is not translated for a general reader. It is translated for a particular notaio, registry or court with its own requirements. That is why I ask who is receiving it before I begin.
