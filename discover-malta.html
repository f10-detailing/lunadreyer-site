Property · Malta

Golden stone, blue water, long summer nights.

Malta is small enough to cross in an hour and rich enough to fill a lifetime: honey-coloured fortress towns, coves of impossibly clear water and village feasts that end in fireworks. English is spoken everywhere, and many Australians already have family here. These are the places that make people stay for the whole summer, and then come back for good.

Six places to fall for

From the capital on its harbour to the quiet green island of Gozo, everything is close, so a morning in a medieval city and an afternoon swim are always the same day.

Malta

Valletta

Built by the Knights of St John on a peninsula above the Grand Harbour, Valletta is a UNESCO World Heritage city of golden limestone, steep streets and colourful wooden balconies. Watch the noon cannon fire from the Upper Barrakka Gardens, see the Caravaggios in St John's Co-Cathedral, then linger over dinner on the steps as the stone glows at dusk.

Malta

Mdina

The old capital, called the Silent City, sits on a hilltop behind medieval walls. Its narrow lanes, palaces and cathedral are almost hushed, and the bastions look out across the whole island to the sea. Next door, Rabat adds bakeries, catacombs and everyday village life.

Gozo

Ramla Bay, Gozo

A short ferry ride brings you to Gozo, greener and slower than Malta. Ramla Bay is its best-loved beach, a sweep of red-gold sand below terraced hills, with shallow water perfect for long summer swims. On the other side of the island, the cliffs of Xlendi Bay shelter a small harbour of seafood restaurants.

South Malta

Marsaxlokk

Malta's great fishing village, where the harbour fills with luzzu boats painted blue, yellow and red, each with a watchful eye on the bow. The Sunday market spills along the waterfront, and the restaurants serve whatever came in that morning, grilled simply with lemon and olive oil.

Malta

Sliema and St Julian's

The lively side of Malta: a long seafront promenade made for evening walks, rocky swimming platforms, cafés and the little fishing harbour of Spinola Bay. From Sliema the ferry crosses to Valletta in minutes, with the capital's skyline filling the view across the water.

Comino

Blue Lagoon, Comino

Between Malta and Gozo lies tiny Comino, almost without cars, and its famous Blue Lagoon: water so clear and turquoise that boats seem to float in the air. Come on an early boat, swim across to the islet of Cominotto and walk the quiet coastal paths.

Summer there, while Melbourne is in winter

June to August is high summer in Malta: hot, dry, sunny days, a warm sea and evenings made for being outdoors.

Sea and sun

Typical Maltese summer days are cloudless and warm, and the sea stays inviting from June well into autumn. Mornings for swimming, long lunches in the shade, afternoons by the water.

Festa season

From June to September each town and village celebrates its patron saint: streets hung with lights and banners, brass bands, processions and fireworks lighting up the sky at night.

Everything close

No place is far. A swim in the morning, a medieval city at lunch and a harbour dinner in the evening, with the ferry to Gozo or a drive across the island in well under an hour.

Living there

Everyday life in Malta is easy for Australians. English is an official language alongside Maltese, you drive on the left, and Melbourne has one of the largest Maltese communities in the world, so many visitors arrive to cousins, old friends and a familiar welcome.

Food is a joy: flaky pastizzi from the corner bakery, ftira bread filled with tomatoes, capers and tuna, the soft little ġbejniet cheeses of Gozo, rabbit stew on Sundays and fish straight from the boats at Marsaxlokk. Local markets sell sweet summer melons, figs and prickly pears.

The pace is warm and sociable. Towns have their own band clubs, parish feasts and seafront benches where neighbours meet every evening. Retirees find a quiet life in Gozo or the villages, while Sliema and Valletta offer theatres, concerts and cafés.

Getting around is simple, with a national bus network, harbour ferries and the regular ferry to Gozo. From Australia, Malta is reached with one stop through the major hubs in the Gulf, Asia or Europe, landing at Malta International Airport close to everything.

Common questions

When is the best time to go to Malta?

For a full summer, June to September brings hot sunny days, a warm sea and the village festa season with its fireworks. That lines up perfectly with the Australian winter. Spring and autumn are milder and quieter, ideal for walking and sightseeing.

Which part of Malta suits a quiet retirement, and which suits city life?

Gozo and the villages of Malta, such as Mdina and Rabat or the southern harbour towns, suit a calm, community-centred life. Sliema, St Julian's and Valletta suit those who want restaurants, culture and the sea within a short walk.

Is it easy to get by in English in Malta?

Very easy. English is an official language alongside Maltese, so signs, menus, shops, doctors and everyday conversation all work in English.

Is it possible to rent in Malta for a season?

Yes. Furnished apartments and village houses are available for stays of a few weeks to several months, in Gozo as well as on Malta, and many Australians spend the whole European summer this way.
