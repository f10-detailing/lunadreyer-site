Your Greek summer is waiting.

Whitewashed harbours, turquoise coves, village feasts under the stars and family tables that stretch late into the night. While Melbourne settles into winter, Greece is at its brightest. These are the places so many Greek Australians call home, and the reasons to spend the season there.

Six places to fall for

From the Ionian Islands to Crete, the Peloponnese, Macedonia and Epirus: the regions Melbourne's Greek community comes from, and some of the loveliest corners of the Mediterranean.

Ionian Islands

Ithaca

Vathy sits around one of the most sheltered natural harbours in the Mediterranean, its pastel houses and tavernas looking onto still, blue water. This is the island of Odysseus: summer days mean swimming from pebble coves, walking old mule paths between villages and an evening volta along the waterfront.

Ionian Islands

Kefalonia

The largest of the Ionian Islands, with the dazzling white sweep of Myrtos beach, the underground lake of Melissani and the painted harbour of Fiskardo. Summer is for boat trips along the coast, local Robola wine and long lunches in the hill villages.

Crete

Chania

Chania's Venetian harbour, lighthouse and lanes of the old town make it one of the most beautiful towns in Greece. Summer brings days at Balos and Elafonisi, walks through the Samaria Gorge and evenings of Cretan food by the water.

Peloponnese

Nafplio

The first capital of modern Greece, Nafplio is all neoclassical mansions, bougainvillea and marble lanes, watched over by the Palamidi fortress. In summer, swim at Arvanitia, take the boat to the Bourtzi and day trip to ancient Epidaurus and Mycenae.

Macedonia

Thessaloniki and Halkidiki

Thessaloniki, Melbourne's sister city, is a lively waterfront city of Byzantine churches, markets and some of the best food in Greece. An hour or so away, the peninsulas of Halkidiki offer pine forests running down to clear, calm bays for the summer.

Epirus

Parga

Parga's colourful houses tumble down to a bay on the Ionian coast, with a Venetian castle above and the tiny island of Panagia just offshore. Summer means sandy beaches, boat trips to the Acheron river and Paxos, and quiet evenings on the harbour.

Summer there, while Melbourne is in winter

Long, golden days

From June to August Greece is typically hot, dry and sunny, with warm seas, cooling breezes on the islands and evenings that stay light until well after nine.

Island hopping

Summer is when the ferry network is at its fullest. Hop between the Ionian Islands, or sail from Piraeus to the Cyclades and Crete, arriving each time to a new harbour.

Panigyria and 15 August

Villages celebrate their saints' days with panigyria: music, dancing and long tables in the square. The biggest of all is Dekapentavgoustos on 15 August, celebrated across Greece.

Living there

Everyday life in Greece revolves around the table. Mornings start with a coffee in the square, the weekly laiki market brings tomatoes, figs, cheeses and olive oil straight from the farms, and dinner rarely begins before nine.

The pace is unhurried and the welcome is genuine. Neighbours greet each other by name, tavernas know their regulars, and for families with roots in Melbourne there is often a cousin, a village and a house full of stories waiting.

Getting around is part of the pleasure: ferries link the islands, intercity buses reach the towns and villages, and domestic flights connect Athens and Thessaloniki with Crete and the islands. From Melbourne, Athens is a single connection away through the main Gulf and Asian hubs.

Common questions

When is the best time to go to Greece?

June to August is the classic Greek summer, with warm seas, long days and every island ferry running. Late May, early June and September are softer and calmer, and they suit walking, swimming and village life just as well.

Which part of Greece suits a quiet retirement, and which suits city life?

For a quiet season, Ithaca, Kefalonia, Parga and the villages of Halkidiki offer slow days by the water. For city life, Thessaloniki and Chania combine waterfront promenades, markets, culture and good services, with beaches a short drive away.

Is it easy to get by in English in Greece?

Yes. English is widely spoken in the islands, coastal towns and cities, and many Greeks have family in Melbourne. A few words of Greek, such as kalimera and efcharistó, are always warmly received.

Is it possible to rent a home in Greece for a season?

Yes. Many Australians rent a house or apartment for one to three months over the European summer. Booking early is wise for July and August, and longer stays outside peak weeks are often easy to arrange directly with owners.
