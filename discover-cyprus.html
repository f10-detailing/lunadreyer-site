Property · Cyprus

The island of long, golden summers.

Cyprus is where the Mediterranean summer seems to last forever: clear warm sea, ancient harbours, pine-scented mountain villages and a table always laid for one more. English is widely spoken and you drive on the left, so Australians feel at home from the first day. These six places show why so many come for the season and decide to stay.

Six places to fall for

From harbour towns on the coast to stone villages high in the Troodos mountains, the island packs beaches, history and wine country into easy driving distance.

West coast

Paphos

A relaxed harbour town where fishing boats bob beneath a small medieval fort and the promenade fills with strollers at sunset. In Kato Paphos, the archaeological park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, holds some of the finest Roman mosaics in the Mediterranean, steps from the sea.

South coast

Limassol

The island's most cosmopolitan city, with a long seafront promenade, a gleaming marina and an old town of lanes around the medieval castle. Days of swimming give way to evenings in tavernas and wine bars, and the vineyards of the hills are less than an hour away.

Troodos mountains

Troodos villages

High in the pine-covered Troodos mountains, the air is fresher and life slows right down. Omodos gathers around a cobbled square and its old monastery, with local wine and sweet treats in every doorway, while Kakopetria follows a river through old stone and timber houses. Painted Byzantine churches hide in the valleys nearby.

South-east coast

Larnaca

Larnaca greets most visitors with Finikoudes, a palm-lined promenade along a sandy town beach with cafés facing the sea. The old Church of Saint Lazarus stands at the heart of town, and on the edge of the city the Larnaca Salt Lake shimmers white, visited by flamingos in the cooler months.

East coast

Protaras and Ayia Napa

The east coast has the island's most dazzling beaches: pale sand and water so clear and shallow you can see your feet far from shore. Fig Tree Bay in Protaras is perfect for gentle swimming, and the sea caves and cliffs of Cape Greco, between Protaras and Ayia Napa, are made for boat trips and snorkelling.

North-west

Akamas peninsula

The wild corner of Cyprus: a protected peninsula of rugged headlands, wildflowers, walking trails and hidden coves. Boats from the little harbour of Latchi carry swimmers to its own Blue Lagoon, and the Avakas Gorge and the Baths of Aphrodite are close by.

Summer there, while Melbourne is in winter

June to August is the heart of a long Cypriot summer, which in practice stretches from May to October.

Sunshine for months

Cyprus is one of the sunniest places in Europe. Typical summer days are hot, dry and cloudless, the sea is warm, and the season runs long into autumn.

Wine in late summer

In late summer Limassol celebrates its wine festival, with local wines to taste, music and dancing in the city's gardens, a joyful toast to the island's ancient winemaking tradition.

Mountain village life

When the coast is at its warmest, the Troodos villages are cooler and greener: shady squares, cherry and apple orchards, small festivals and long dinners under the vines.

Living there

Everyday life in Cyprus feels familiar to Australians. English is widely spoken, you drive on the left, and there is a long tradition of family ties between Cyprus and Australia, especially in Melbourne.

The table is the centre of everything. A meze can run to a dozen small dishes: grilled halloumi, souvlaki, slow-cooked kleftiko, village salads, fresh fish and sweet watermelon to finish. Municipal markets and roadside stalls overflow with figs, grapes and oranges.

The pace is unhurried and sociable. Coffee in the village square, a swim before lunch, a quiet afternoon and a stroll along the promenade as the evening cools. Retirees love the calm of the Troodos villages or the smaller towns around Paphos, while Limassol and Larnaca offer city energy by the sea.

Distances are short: the main coastal towns are linked by fast motorways and intercity buses, and a car opens up the mountains and wild coast. From Australia, Larnaca and Paphos airports are reached with one stop through the major hubs in the Gulf or Europe.

Common questions

When is the best time to go to Cyprus?

The summer runs from May to October, so the Australian winter is perfect for long beach days and a warm sea. Spring and autumn are milder and ideal for walking in the Troodos mountains and the Akamas peninsula.

Which part of Cyprus suits a quiet retirement, and which suits city life?

The Troodos villages and the smaller towns and villages around Paphos suit a peaceful, community-centred life. Limassol and Larnaca suit those who want restaurants, culture and the seafront on their doorstep.

Is it easy to get by in English in Cyprus?

Yes. English is widely spoken in shops, restaurants, banks and medical clinics, and road signs are in Greek and English.

Is it possible to rent in Cyprus for a season?

Yes. Furnished apartments and village houses are available for stays of a few weeks to several months, both on the coast and in the mountains, so spending a whole summer there is easy to arrange.
