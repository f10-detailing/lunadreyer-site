Before you order a certificate translation

For a birth, marriage or death certificate, check first whether the issuing registry can produce an international or multilingual version of it. Many receiving authorities specifically want that format, and will not accept a translation of the standard domestic certificate at all. If the international version is not available, or the authority you are dealing with does not ask for it, we can translate the standard certificate instead. Ask us before you order the certificate if you are not sure which applies to you.

A translation does not make a qualification equivalent

We can translate a degree, transcript or school record, but the translation itself does not make your studies equivalent to a local qualification. The institution or authority you are applying to will very likely still run its own recognition process on top of the translation, such as an equivalence assessment, additional modules or specific coursework, before your qualification is treated as equivalent. We can tell you what we know, but the receiving institution always has the final say.

Languages, and what NAATI has to do with it

Luna Dreyer, our founder, works in Spanish and English in both directions, and into English from German, Italian, Portuguese and Catalan. Greek, Cypriot Greek, Maltese and French work, and additional Italian capacity, is handled by our collaborators, each working in their own language pair.

We do not provide NAATI-certified translations. NAATI is the Australian accreditation system, and some receiving authorities, most often an Australian government department accepting a foreign document, specifically require a NAATI-certified translation. If that is what you need, this is not the right service, and we will tell you so rather than take the work on. For documents going the other way, or to a destination that sets its own requirements rather than asking for a NAATI stamp, we can help. If you are not sure which applies to you, ask before you pay for anything.

Pricing and turnaround

Official and personal documents for use overseas start from $69 per page including GST (one page is 250 words). Business, legal and financial documents start from $25 per 100 words plus GST, with a minimum job of $60 plus GST. These match the rates published by established Australian agencies. Every job is confirmed in a fixed written quote before work begins.

Turnaround is usually a few business days, depending on length and complexity, and longer if notarisation or an apostille is also involved. Notary and DFAT fees are charged separately by those offices.

Common questions

Do you do NAATI-certified translations?

No. We do not provide NAATI-certified translations. If that is specifically what you need, we are not the right agency for it, and we will say so upfront.

Can you translate my birth, marriage or death certificate?

Often, yes, but check first whether the issuing registry offers an international or multilingual version, since many receiving authorities want that instead of a translation. If the international version does not apply to your situation, we can translate the standard certificate.

Will a translated degree be recognised overseas?

The translation lets the receiving institution read your degree or transcript. It does not by itself make your qualification equivalent to a local one. Expect the institution to run its own recognition process as well.

How is the price worked out?
