Translation services

Translation for documents that have to work somewhere else.

Most of my work is Spanish and English, in both directions. I also translate into English from German, Italian, Portuguese and Catalan. The common thread is documents that have to hold up with a specific reader: a registry, a bank, an institution or a court, not a general audience.

Before you order a certificate translation

For a birth, marriage or death certificate, check first whether the issuing registry can produce an international or multilingual version of it. Many receiving authorities specifically want that format, and will not accept a translation of the standard domestic certificate at all. If the international version is not available, or the authority you are dealing with does not ask for it, I can translate the standard certificate instead. Ask me before you order the certificate if you are not sure which applies to you.

A translation does not make a qualification equivalent

I can translate a degree, transcript or school record, but the translation itself does not make your studies equivalent to a local qualification. The institution or authority you are applying to will very likely still run its own recognition process on top of the translation — an equivalence assessment, additional modules, or specific coursework — before your qualification is treated as equivalent. I can tell you what I know, but the receiving institution always has the final say.

Languages, and what NAATI has to do with it

Most of my work is Spanish and English, in both directions. I can also translate into English from German, Italian, Portuguese and Catalan.

I am not NAATI certified. NAATI is the Australian accreditation system, and some receiving authorities — most often an Australian government department accepting a foreign document — specifically require a NAATI-certified translation. If that is what you need, this is not the right service, and I will tell you so rather than take the work on. For documents going the other way, or to a destination that sets its own requirements rather than asking for a NAATI stamp, I can help. If you are not sure which applies to you, ask before you pay for anything.

Pricing and turnaround

Every document is different, so I quote a fixed price once I have seen what needs translating, rather than a standing per-word rate. Turnaround is usually a few business days, depending on length and complexity, and longer if notarisation or an apostille is also involved. Tell me what the document is and where it is going, and I will give you a clear quote before any work begins.

Common questions

Do you do NAATI-certified translations?

No. I am not NAATI accredited. If a NAATI-certified translation is specifically what you need, I am not the right person for it, and I will say so upfront.

Can you translate my birth, marriage or death certificate?

Often, yes, but check first whether the issuing registry offers an international or multilingual version, since many receiving authorities want that instead of a translation. If the international version does not apply to your situation, I can translate the standard certificate.

Will a translated degree be recognised overseas?

The translation lets the receiving institution read your degree or transcript. It does not by itself make your qualification equivalent to a local one. Expect the institution to run its own recognition process as well.

How is the price worked out?

By the document: what it is, its length, and any notarisation or apostille steps involved. I quote a fixed price upfront rather than charging by the word.
